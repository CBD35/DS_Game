## World Overview

Athas was once a verdant world. It is no longer.

Centuries of defiler magic have drained the life force from soil, forest, and river. Grasslands became scrubland; scrubland became dust; rivers became cracked clay. The sun burns a deep red-orange, hotter and crueler than any natural star. Water is the most valuable commodity on the planet. A full waterskin is worth more than most weapons.

Seven city-states dominate the habitable portions of the Tablelands, each ruled by an immortal Sorcerer-King of terrifying power. Between them lie hundreds of miles of desert, badlands, and salt flat where survival is the only law. The institution of slavery underpins every city-state economy. The Veiled Alliance — a network of Preserver wizards — fights in the shadows to resist the Sorcerer-Kings. The Dragon of Tyr, a being of catastrophic power, demands periodic tribute in slaves.

Athas is not a backdrop. It is a dying organism, and every player action either slows or accelerates that death.

## The Dying Sun

Athas's sun burns red-orange — what scholars call the **Crimson Phase**. It did not always. Ancient texts describe a yellow sun. The shift correlates precisely with the rise of defiler magic over three thousand years.

### Heat Stages (Mechanical)
| Stage | Time of Day | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Dawn | 05:00–07:00 | Safe; no penalty |
| Morning | 07:00–11:00 | Mild; no penalty |
| Midday | 11:00–15:00 | Hot; 1 heat damage per hour unprotected |
| Peak | 13:00–14:00 | Dangerous; 2 heat damage per hour; Constitution save DC 12 or Fatigued |
| Afternoon | 15:00–18:00 | Cooling; back to Midday conditions |
| Dusk/Night | 18:00–05:00 | Safe from heat; Cold Exposure risk in deep wastes |

- Shade or full cover negates heat damage
- Water consumption doubles during Midday/Peak
- The **Scorched** condition (prolonged exposure without water): -2 to all attacks, -1 AC, max HP reduced by 10%

## History of Athas

### The Blue Age (Ancient — ~15,000 years before present)
Athas was dominated by a global ocean. The Rhulisti (halflings) were the dominant civilization, practitioners of **Life-shaping** — a biological form of magic that created organisms for specific purposes. The sun was blue-white.

### The Green Age (~3,000–13,000 years before present)
Life-shaping accidentally created beings capable of arcane magic. These beings — the first humanoid races — spread across Athas as the oceans retreated. The world was verdant: forests, rivers, grasslands. Magic was practiced exclusively by **Preservers** who drew arcane energy without harming the environment. Psionics was universal.

**Key Developments:**
- Rise of the humanoid races (humans, elves, dwarves, and others)
- The great city-states of the Green Age — trading, cultural, peaceful
- The Psionic Academies — formalized training; psionics became diplomatic currency

### The Cleansing Wars (~3,000 years before present)
The wizard **Rajaat** discovered the secret of defiler magic and began the **Cleansing Wars** — a genocide campaign to exterminate all non-halfling races and restore the Blue Age. He trained fourteen champions in defiler magic. These champions, called the **Champions of Rajaat**, became the Sorcerer-Kings.

Eventually the champions turned on Rajaat and imprisoned him in a pocket dimension called the **Hollow** — but they kept the defiler power and the city-states they had carved out.

**Key Figures:**
- **Rajaat the Warbringer** — original Defiler; imprisoned but not destroyed
- **Borys of Ebe** — greatest of the Champions; became the Dragon; now demands tribute
- **Kalak** — Sorcerer-King of Tyr; relevant to the game's present-day events

### The Age of the Sorcerer-Kings (~3,000 years before present to present)
The seven surviving Champions established permanent dominion over the Tablelands. Their sustained defilement progressively destroyed Athas's ecology. They now maintain immortality through ritual defilement on a massive scale.

**Present day:** The game begins approximately 10 years after Kalak of Tyr was publicly assassinated by a coalition of gladiators, templars, and Veiled Alliance members — an event unprecedented in Athasian history.

## Geography — The Tablelands

The Tablelands is the primary continent of Athas and the setting of Shattered Lands. It is a vast elevated plateau surrounded by lower terrain. The game covers the central and northern Tablelands.

### Major Regions

#### The Ringing Mountains (West)
The tallest peaks on the Tablelands. Source of the last significant rainfall. Some green valleys survive on north-facing slopes. Heavily contested for their water and timber.

#### The Forest Ridge (Far West — beyond game scope)
A massive tropical forest beyond the Ringing Mountains. Known only by rumor in the game's setting. Referenced in lore but not visited.

#### The Sea of Silt (East)
A vast basin filled not with water but with ultra-fine grey silt. Impassable without special equipment. Silt Runners (small reptilian scavengers) and massive Silt Horrors lurk beneath the surface. The eastern city-states border it.

#### The Tablelands Proper (Central)
Scrubland, badlands, dry riverbeds, and dust. Most overland travel occurs here. Rocky buttes, obsidian plains, and crumbling ruins of Green Age civilization dot the landscape.

#### The Tyr Region (North-Central — primary play area)
The Tyr Region is the core play area of Shattered Lands. It contains Tyr, Urik, Raam, Draj, and the major wilderness areas between them.

## The City-States

Seven city-states dominate the Tablelands. Each is a fortress of civilization in the desert — and a monument to the power that is killing the world.

### Tyr — City of Freedom
- **Former Ruler:** Sorcerer-King **Kalak** (deceased — killed ~10 years before game events)
- **Current Status:** Under a fragile council government; the only free city-state on Athas
- **Economy:** Iron mines (rare — Tyr sits on the continent's only known iron deposit), agriculture, trade
- **Population:** ~15,000 free citizens, formerly ~30,000 slaves (emancipation in progress, chaotic)
- **Significance:** The game's primary base of operations. The assassination of Kalak is the living memory that defines current Athasian politics.
- **Notable Locations:** The Golden Tower (former Kalak's palace, now council chambers), the Elven Market, the Warrens, the Stadium of Tyr (arena, still operational)
- **Remake Additions:** Expanded city districts, named merchants, Veiled Alliance safehouse questline, political instability events

### Urik — City of Lions
- **Ruler:** Sorcerer-King **Hamanu**, the Lion of Urik — militaristic, disciplined, publicly respected
- **Economy:** Iron weapons manufacture (trades with Tyr), military campaigns, slave labor
- **Population:** ~20,000 free, ~50,000 slaves
- **Character:** The most orderly of the city-states. Hamanu is genuinely competent at governance; his cruelty is strategic, not sadistic.
- **Remake Additions:** Expanded Hamanu characterization; Urik military questline; moral dilemma arc about whether Hamanu is the "lesser evil"

### Raam — City of Chaos
- **Ruler:** Sorcerer-King **Abalach-Re**, the Great Queen — increasingly unstable, her Templar network fragmenting
- **Economy:** Trade hub, mercenary guilds, narcotics
- **Population:** ~18,000 free, ~45,000 slaves — but internal power struggles make census meaningless
- **Character:** The most dangerous city-state to visit. No reliable authority; multiple power factions war openly in the streets.
- **Remake Additions:** Raam destabilization questline; opportunity to accelerate or prevent Abalach-Re's collapse

### Draj — City of the Moon Priests
- **Ruler:** Sorcerer-King **Tectuktitlay**, Father of Life and Master of the Two Moons — presents as a god-king of an Aztec-flavored culture
- **Economy:** Agricultural (fertile margin land), human sacrifice, drug production
- **Population:** ~12,000 free, ~35,000 slaves
- **Character:** Deeply religious theocracy in Tectuktitlay's constructed mythology. Templars are "moon priests." Sacrifice is genuine, meaningful, and frequent.
- **Remake Additions:** Expanded Draj cultural content; moral weight on the sacrifice system; secret Veiled Alliance cell

### Gulg — City of the Forest
- **Ruler:** Sorcerer-King **Lalali-Puy**, the Forest Goddess, the Oba
- **Economy:** Forest products, hunting, unique fauna
- **Population:** ~10,000 free, ~20,000 slaves
- **Character:** Semi-tropical village/city built into a surviving forest. Lalali-Puy has maintained the forest through selective Preservation — she is unusual among Sorcerer-Kings. Her cruelty is intimate rather than industrial.
- **Game Relevance:** Gulg is reachable in Act 2; its forest is a key resource in the main plot

### Nibenay — City of Spires
- **Ruler:** Sorcerer-King **Nibenay**, the Shadow King — reclusive, scholarly, occasionally brilliant
- **Economy:** Crafts, trade, psionic research
- **Population:** ~14,000 free, ~30,000 slaves
- **Character:** The most "civilized" of the city-states from the outside. Nibenay retreats into his palace for years at a time; his many Templar-wives run the city.
- **Game Relevance:** Nibenay's psionic research connects to a key subplot in the remake's expanded content

### Balic — City of Sails
- **Ruler:** Sorcerer-King **Andropinis** — elected dictator of a nominally republican city-state
- **Economy:** Sea of Silt trade, silt skimmer commerce, olive oil
- **Population:** ~13,000 free, ~28,000 slaves
- **Character:** Maintains a fiction of democracy — citizens vote, senate meets, elections are held. Andropinis has won every election for 400 years. The performance of freedom makes the slavery somehow worse.

## The Sorcerer-Kings

The Sorcerer-Kings are not simply powerful wizards. They are former Champions of Rajaat who have sustained themselves for three thousand years through:

1. **Mass Defilement:** Periodic ritual defilement of large land areas; each king maintains a "defilement reserve" — land they systematically destroy for power
2. **Dragon Metamorphosis (partial):** Each has begun the transformation into a Dragon — a process that grants immortality but requires escalating defilement. Only Borys (the Dragon) has completed it.
3. **Psionic Mastery:** All Sorcerer-Kings are 20th-level Defilers AND high-level Psionicists. Engagement in direct combat is a wipe; the game never asks players to fight them directly until the final confrontation.
4. **Templar Network:** Templars are granted limited psionic power directly from their king's reserves. Killing the king severs this power — a major political consequence.

## The Wilderness

The Tablelands between city-states are hostile in every dimension: heat, predators, bandit activity, and political patrols.

### Travel System
- World map is node-based with wilderness areas between nodes
- Travel consumes **water** (primary), **food** (secondary), and **time**
- Random encounters generated from regional tables (Hostile / Environmental / Opportunity / Lore)
- Party can **push** (faster, more Fatigue) or **pace** (slower, resource-conservative)

### Kank Mounts
Kanks are giant insect mounts used for desert travel. They produce small amounts of water from nectary organs — a significant survival benefit.
- Carrying capacity: 300 lbs per kank
- Speed bonus: +30% travel speed
- Can be killed in combat; stabling costs money in cities

Athas's surviving fauna are the products of extreme evolutionary pressure. Most are dangerous. Many are psionic.

### Flora
- **Spiny shrubs** — thorned drought-adapted scrub; ubiquitous
- **Carbo tree** — deep-rooted; small edible fruit; water source if root tapped (DC 16 Survival)
- **Ashen weed** — grows only where defilement has occurred; Veiled Alliance uses it as a tracking indicator
- **Broy-vine** — produces a fermentable fruit; common in Gulg's forest margin

## The Absence of Metal

Iron and steel are functionally extinct on the surface of Athas. The last major surface deposits were exhausted in the Cleansing Wars.

**Exception:** Tyr's iron mines are the only known active surface iron source on the Tablelands — which is a primary reason multiple Sorcerer-Kings have historically tried to conquer Tyr.

### Material Hierarchy
| Material | Typical Uses | Fragility | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Obsidian | Blades, arrowheads | High (chips) | Razor-sharp; brittle on impact |
| Bone | Hafts, clubs, blades | Medium | Flexible; available everywhere |
| Chitin | Armor, shields | Medium | Light; giant insects are primary source |
| Stone | Hammers, maces | Low | Heavy; durable blunt weapons |
| Hardwood | Hafts, staves, shields | Medium | Scarce; valuable |
| Crystal | Blades, psionic foci | Low | Rare; psionic-conductive; high damage |
| Iron/Steel | Any | None | Priceless; Tyr-origin only; never craftable |

### Fragility System
All non-metal weapons have a Fragility Rating (1–5). On a natural 1 attack roll: roll d6; if ≤ Fragility Rating, weapon gains a Crack (–1 damage). Two cracks = destroyed. Repair requires materials + Craft check.

## Political Factions

### The Templars
The administrative and enforcement arm of each Sorcerer-King. Templars draw psionic power from their king — they are not naturally psionic. They range from corrupt enforcers to genuine civil administrators. They are not uniformly evil; they are uniformly complicit.

### The Veiled Alliance
Underground network of Preserver wizards and sympathizers. Their goal: overthrow the Sorcerer-Kings and restore Preserving magic as the dominant practice. Internal factions range from principled pacifists to those willing to level a city block to prove a point. They are a potential ally — not a savior.

### The Merchant Houses
Three major Houses (House Wavir, House Shom, House Inika) control long-distance trade. They sell to everyone, including Sorcerer-Kings. Their caravans are the only reliable long-distance news network. They prize neutrality because it's profitable. Crossing them is inadvisable.

### The Elemental Cults
Earth, Air, Fire, Water — four cults, often in conflict with each other. Elemental clerics draw genuine power from elemental nodes scattered across Athas. They are fragmented but united in opposition to the Sorcerer-Kings, whose defilement degrades the elemental fabric.

### The Dragon
Borys of Ebe — the only Sorcerer-King to have completed the Dragon metamorphosis. He now demands a tribute of 1,000 slaves per year from the city-states, collectively. His purpose: feeding Rajaat's prison. The Dragon is not a villain in the traditional sense — he is a catastrophe, like a hurricane. He cannot be fought. He can only be planned around.
