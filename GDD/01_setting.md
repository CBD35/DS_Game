## World Overview

Kharak'thal was once a verdant world. It is no longer.

Centuries of defiler magic have stripped the world's life force from the soil. Forests became scrubland; scrubland became dust; dust became the Crimson Wastes. The sun — once yellow — now burns a deep arterial red, its light hotter and harsher than any natural star. Water is the most valuable commodity on the planet. A full waterskin is worth more than a sword.

The world is not dead. It is dying. Slowly, stubbornly, with the last reserves of something that once made it extraordinary. Pockets of life cling to oases, underground aquifers, and the margins of mountain ranges. Creatures have adapted to brutality — or they have not, and they are gone. The survivors are uniformly dangerous.

Seven city-states dominate the inhabited portions of Kharak'thal, each ruled by an immortal Sorcerer-King who maintains power through arcane might, psychic domination, and the institution of slavery. Between the city-states lie the Crimson Wastes: hundreds of miles of desert, scrub, salt flat, and shattered badlands where the only law is survival.

## The Dying Sun

The sun of Kharak'thal is called **Khet** in the oldest texts. It burns with a red-orange light that climatologists in the game's world call "the Bleeding" — a phenomenon that has been worsening for three centuries.

### Mechanical Effects
- **Heat Stages:** Morning (bearable), Midday (dangerous), Afternoon Peak (lethal without shade or water).
- **UV Damage:** Prolonged exposure without protection causes the Scorched condition, reducing max HP and imposing -2 to all attack rolls.
- **Night Travel:** Preferred by most Waste travelers. Night temperatures drop sharply (Cold Exposure risk), but heat hazard is eliminated.
- **Psionic Resonance:** Khet's red light carries a faint psionic frequency. Psionicists gain +1 to all psionic attack rolls during full-sun hours but suffer more severe Wild Surge results.

### Lore
Ancient texts describe Khet as once being gold. The change correlates precisely with the rise of defiler wizardry. Some scholars argue causation; others argue correlation. The Sorcerer-Kings suppress both hypotheses.

## History

### The Green Age (~3,000 years before present)
Kharak'thal was a living world: forests, rivers, grasslands, and diverse civilizations. Magic existed but was practiced exclusively by Preservers — wizards who drew arcane energy without damaging the environment. Psionics was universal; nearly every sapient being had some latent ability.

**Key Events:**
- The Founding of the First Cities — agrarian civilization, barter economies, elemental worship
- The Concordat of Waters — international water-sharing agreements between early city-states
- The First Psionic Academies — systematized psionic training; the **Psions of the Concordat** become diplomatic power brokers

### The Unraveling (~1,500 years before present)
A single wizard — known only as **The Architect** — discovered that by drawing arcane energy from living matter rather than ambient sources, spells became enormously more powerful. He shared this discovery with six others. These Seven Defilers became the founders of the Sorcerer-King dynasties.

**Key Events:**
- The First Defilement — the Architect destroys a forest the size of a province in a single ritual
- The War of Scales — conventional armies prove useless against Defiler magic; within a generation, the Seven control the major population centers
- The Breaking of the Concordat — water-sharing agreements collapse as the Defilers redirect rivers to consolidate power
- First appearance of the **Veiled Alliance** — Preserver resistance movement, driven underground

### The Long Drought (~1,000–500 years before present)
The consequences of sustained defilement begin manifesting at continental scale. Rivers dry up. Forests collapse to scrubland. Grasslands become dust. The Sorcerer-Kings, aware of what they are doing, accelerate rather than stop — power requires it.

**Key Events:**
- The Death of the Keth River — the last major waterway in the central continent fails
- The Great Extinction — over 200 species of megafauna disappear within three generations
- The Establishment of the Arena System — Sorcerer-Kings formalize gladiatorial combat as social control and entertainment
- The **Salt Wars** — three city-states war over a newly discovered salt flat; two are destroyed; survivors absorbed

### The Crimson Age (500 years before present to present)
The world as players know it. The sun has bled red for four centuries. Metal has become functionally extinct in the surface world. The Sorcerer-Kings have achieved a form of conditional immortality through ritual and sustained defilement. Population is declining. The city-states are the only stable civilization.

**Key Events:**
- The Crimson Rising — the sun's shift to red; mass extinction of remaining surface fauna
- The Closing of the Deep Roads — dwarven underground trade routes collapse after aquifer failure
- The Ssurrai Emergence — the original race (see 02_races.md) appears in the deep wastes; origin unknown
- **Present Day:** Players enter the narrative. Something is accelerating the world's death.

## Geography

### Major Regions

#### The Crimson Wastes (Central)
The vast uninhabited desert at the world's heart. 900+ miles of eroded stone, deep sand, and salt flat. Daytime travel is suicidal without preparation. Home to the largest and most dangerous megafauna — creatures that have adapted to total resource scarcity through size, aggression, and psionic ability.

- **Terrain:** Sand seas (ergs), rock plateaus (regs), dry lake beds (playas), canyon systems
- **Hazards:** Extreme heat, sand storms (3-day visibility zero events), sink sand, psionic predators
- **Notable Features:** The Shattered Spire (ancient Preserver academy, now ruin), the Obsidian Shelf (natural glass plain, reflective heat amplifier)

#### The Scorch Belt (Surrounding Wastes)
A transitional zone of scrubland, dried riverbeds, and badlands. Most overland travel between city-states passes through here. Moderate danger. Bandit activity is highest in this region.

- **Terrain:** Cracked clay, dry washes, thorn scrub, scattered rock formations
- **Hazards:** Moderate heat, bandit ambushes, territorial herd animals
- **Notable Features:** The Amber Road (ancient trade route, partially navigable), the Bonefield (mass grave from the Salt Wars, now a landmark)

#### The Keth Margin (North)
The desiccated bed of the former Keth River. Dotted with deep wells and underground springs — the most contested real estate on the continent. Multiple city-states maintain military outposts here.

- **Terrain:** Wide flat lowlands, dried riverbeds, occasional standing water at deep springs
- **Hazards:** Political — every spring is claimed; trespass is a capital offense in most city-states
- **Notable Features:** The Deepwell of Karrath (neutral meeting ground by ancient treaty), the Keth Ruins (drowned cities now exposed by evaporation)

#### The Iron Fingers (West)
A mountain range of eroded volcanic plugs — "fingers" of basalt jutting from the desert floor. Contains the last significant forests on the continent: hardy drought-resistant species on north-facing slopes.

- **Terrain:** Volcanic rock, sparse forest, hidden valleys with year-round springs
- **Hazards:** Altitude cold, unstable volcanic activity (minor), elven clan territory
- **Notable Features:** The Verdant Pocket (largest surviving forest, fiercely protected by Preservers), Forge Mountain (dormant volcano, source of the continent's remaining obsidian)

#### The Salt Flats (East)
Blinding white salt desert, legacy of an ancient inland sea. Almost entirely uninhabited. Crossing it is a rite of passage for Thri-Kreen hunting parties.

- **Terrain:** Flat salt crust over unstable brine mud, zero shade, perfect reflectivity
- **Hazards:** Heat doubled by reflection, brine seepage (contaminates water supplies), disorientation (no landmarks), Thri-Kreen territorial rights
- **Notable Features:** The Mirror of Sek (deepest point, occasional mirages with psionic properties)

## City-States

Seven city-states survive on Kharak'thal, each a fortress of civilization in the desert. Each is ruled by a Sorcerer-King of immense power and ancient age. The city-states are not allies — they are rivals restrained from open war by the mutual understanding that total war would destroy them all.

### Var-Khet (Primary Setting, Acts 1–2)
- **Ruler:** Sorcerer-King **Serath the Unbroken**, presented as benevolent, actually operating a decades-long immortality scheme
- **Economy:** Gladiatorial tourism, salt trade, slave labor agriculture
- **Population:** ~12,000 free citizens, ~40,000 slaves
- **Notable Districts:** The Arena Quarter, the Templar Fortress, the Dust Warrens (slave quarters), the Merchant Tier
- **Special Feature:** The Psionic Suppression Grid — a network of obsidian obelisks that dampen psionic ability within city walls (limits arena escapes; players must disable it)

### Desh-Arak (Act 2–3 hub)
- **Ruler:** Sorcerer-King **Morthek the Hollow** — a near-corpse sustained by a life-draining throne
- **Economy:** Water brokerage — controls the Deepwell of Karrath
- **Population:** ~8,000 free, ~25,000 slaves
- **Special Feature:** The most active Veiled Alliance cell on the continent operates here under Morthek's nose

### The Five Others (Background)
Karath-Sul, Ossivere, Dun-Makhet, Ssarak's Reach, and the Dead City of Veleth (destroyed 200 years ago, now a ruin-state governed by a mad lich who may or may not be a former Sorcerer-King).

## The Wilderness

Between city-states, there is no law, no water, and no mercy.

### Wilderness Travel System
Travel between nodes on the world map consumes:
- **Water:** Primary survival resource. 1 unit per character per day minimum; 2 in heat stages.
- **Food:** Secondary. Characters can survive 3 days without food before stat penalties begin.
- **Time:** Days of travel tracked. Party can push (move faster, more exhaustion) or pace (slower, less resource burn).

### Random Encounter Tables
Wilderness encounters are generated from regional tables. Categories:
- **Hostile:** Creatures, bandit parties, Templar patrols
- **Environmental:** Sandstorms, flash floods (rare), heat events, psionic anomalies
- **Opportunity:** Ruins with salvage, stranded travelers, Veiled Alliance drop sites
- **Lore:** Ancient inscriptions, bones with identifiable equipment, environmental storytelling

## Ecology

Kharak'thal's surviving fauna are uniformly dangerous. Evolution in resource scarcity has produced creatures with extreme aggression, efficient water storage, and — in many cases — psionic capability.

### Megafauna
| Creature | Size | Threat | Psionic? | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Khet-Drake | Huge | CR 12 | No | Flying reptile; hunts by heat signature |
| Sand Wurm | Gargantuan | CR 16 | No | Burrowing ambush predator; detects vibration |
| Psion-Spider | Large | CR 8 | Yes | Telepathic ambush; induces hallucinations |
| Mul-Crusher | Large | CR 10 | No | Six-limbed pack hunter; targets large prey |
| Dust Wraith | Medium | CR 9 | Yes | Semi-corporeal; feeds on psionic energy |
| Bone Strider | Huge | CR 11 | No | Carrion feeder; territorial, not aggressive unless threatened |

### Flora
Surviving plants are almost entirely thorned, toxic, or both. Notable species:
- **Bloodthorn:** Desert shrub with inch-long spines; sap is a mild poison and component in several alchemical recipes
- **Skullmoss:** Grey-green ground cover found near water; edible, tasteless, valuable
- **Wyrmroot:** Deep taproot plant; the root contains 2d4 units of potable water per adult plant; locating it requires Survival skill check (DC 14)
- **Ashweed:** Indicator species — grows only where defiler magic has been used; used by Preservers to map defilement spread

## The Absence of Metal

Iron, steel, copper, bronze — all are essentially gone from the surface world. The reasons are both historical (exhausted by pre-Cataclysm civilization) and practical (the few remaining deposits are under Sorcerer-King control).

### Material Hierarchy
| Material | Hardness | Weight | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Obsidian | Brittle, very sharp | Medium | Chips easily; best cutting weapons; volcanic origin |
| Bone | Moderate | Light | Ubiquitous; carves well; flexible; blunt-force applications |
| Chitin | Hard, light | Light | Harvested from giant insects; excellent armor material |
| Stone (flint/chert) | Hard, heavy | Heavy | Crude but reliable; blunt and cutting |
| Wood (hardwood) | Moderate | Medium | Scarce; premium material; hafting and shields |
| Crystal | Hard, very sharp | Light | Rare; psionic-conductive; valuable |
| Iron/Steel | Hardest, durable | Heavy | Effectively priceless; possession marks extreme wealth or Sorcerer-King favor |

### Crafting Philosophy
- No metal = No plate armor. Maximum armor is chitin-and-hide equivalent (AC 14 base).
- Weapons break. All non-metal weapons have a **Fragility Rating** (1–5). On a natural 1 attack roll, roll d6; if result ≤ Fragility Rating, weapon gains a crack (–1 damage). Two cracks = destroyed.
- Repair requires appropriate materials and a Craft skill check.
- Metal items, if found, can never be crafted by players — only looted or purchased. They never break on natural 1s.

## Political Structure

### The Sorcerer-Kings
Seven immortal wizard-kings who rule through a combination of:
1. **Arcane power** — each is a 20th-level Defiler at minimum
2. **Psionic capability** — all have at least Telepathy discipline mastery
3. **Templar network** — bureaucratic enforcers granted limited psionic power by their king
4. **Slave economy** — 60–80% of city-state populations are enslaved; free citizens are complicit in their own pacification

### Templars
The administrative and enforcement arm of each Sorcerer-King. Not all Templars are evil — some are bureaucrats, tax collectors, road wardens. All are complicit. All draw their psionic ability from their king's power.

### The Veiled Alliance
Underground network of Preserver wizards and sympathizers. Not uniformly heroic — internal factions range from principled resistance to terrorist cells willing to destroy a city to prove a point. The Alliance is a potential ally, not a savior.

### Merchant Houses
Three major Houses control long-distance trade between city-states. They are nominally neutral — they sell to everyone, including slavers, and their caravans are the only reliable news network in the world. Crossing a Merchant House is inadvisable.

### Elemental Cults
Worshippers of the four elements (Earth, Air, Fire, Water). Not gods — the elements are real, accessible forces. Cult clerics draw genuine power from elemental nodes. The cults are fragmented, often in conflict with each other, but united in opposition to the Sorcerer-Kings who defile the elemental fabric of the world.
