## Story Philosophy

The original Shattered Lands told a focused, uncompromising story: slaves escape, learn the stakes, and face an impossible situation with no clean options. The remake preserves every beat of that arc and extends it — completing the story threads the original left dangling, deepening the characters who deserved more, and closing the loops that the sequel Wake of the Ravager never fully addressed.

**Nothing is retconned.** Where the original made a choice, the remake honors it. Where the original ended abruptly or suggested content that wasn't developed, the remake finishes the job.

### Tone Commitments
- No alignment system; no "good route." Every significant choice costs something.
- Slavery is the economic foundation of Athas. The game does not sanitize it. Resisting it has real costs.
- The Sorcerer-Kings are not mustache-twirling villains. They are ancient, intelligent, and operating according to a logic the player gradually understands — and finds horrifying precisely because it makes a certain kind of sense.
- The Dragon is not a boss fight. The Dragon is a fact of Athas, like the heat.

## Act One — The Arena

### Opening
The party begins as slaves in the arena of **Tyr** — or, in the original game's setup, at a city-state holding facility en route to Tyr. The remake preserves the original opening: waking in a slave pen, being marched to the arena, fighting for survival with whatever weapons are provided.

**The original opening is preserved beat-for-beat:**
- Waking in the pens
- The first arena fight (tutorial combat)
- Meeting the other slaves who will become potential companions
- The unexpected event that breaks the routine (in the original: a coordinated escape attempt during a bout)

### The Escape
The escape from the arena is the inciting event. In the original, this is triggered by an external event (a riot, a power failure, or a coordinated slave uprising depending on version). The remake clarifies and expands this:

- The escape is triggered by **Rikus** (a canonical Mul gladiator and named companion) who has been planning it for months
- The player's party assists — their actions during the opening arena bouts determined whether Rikus trusts them enough to include them
- The escape takes the party through the gladiatorial quarters, the city's slave district, and out through an aqueduct
- The Templar response is immediate; the party cannot fight their way through — they must run

### Act One Stakes
The party escapes Tyr with nothing: no equipment beyond what they grabbed during the escape, no food, no water plan, and Templar pursuit behind them. The immediate goal is survival. The larger goal — which they don't know yet — is just beginning to take shape.

### Act One Key Locations
- **The Arena of Tyr:** Tutorial environment; limited but introduces combat, arena rules, party management
- **The Slave Warrens:** Stealth/social segment; meet key NPCs; gather intel on the escape
- **The Aqueduct:** Chase sequence; environmental hazards; introduces survival mechanics
- **The Scrub Outside Tyr:** First wilderness segment; survival pressure begins immediately

### Act One Ending
The party reaches the ruins of a small farming village that has been defiled — ash rings everywhere, nothing living. This is the first environmental storytelling beat: whoever caused this was powerful, and they were here recently. Act Two begins.

## Act Two — The Tablelands

### Structure
Act Two is the open portion of the game — the largest act, covering travel across the Tablelands, engagement with multiple city-states, and the unraveling of the main conspiracy. The remake expands Act Two significantly vs the original, which was compressed.

### The Main Thread
The party learns that Tyr's old Sorcerer-King **Kalak** did not simply die. His death triggered the release of something he was containing — a ritual he had been building for decades that, now without its anchor, is continuing automatically. The ritual is designed to drain an entire region's life force to complete Kalak's metamorphosis into a Dragon-equivalent. It cannot be stopped from Tyr.

To understand and ultimately stop it, the party must:
1. Find the Veiled Alliance cell in Desh-Arak, who have records of Kalak's ritual structure
2. Locate the three anchor points of the ritual (scattered across the Tablelands)
3. Decide what to do with that information — and who to trust

### Act Two: City-State Visits

#### Tyr (Revisit)
The party can return to Tyr in Act Two under the radar. Tyr is now in political upheaval — the council is fracturing, the Templar network has lost its power source (Kalak's death severed their ability), and former slaves are organizing. Subquests:
- **The Templar Fracture:** A rogue Templar faction is trying to install a new Sorcerer-King; the Veiled Alliance wants them stopped; the player can choose sides or play both
- **The Arena Returns:** Optional; the Arena of Tyr is now run by a council committee; fights continue; reputation and gold available
- **Rikus's People:** If Rikus is a companion, his personal quest advances here

#### Desh-Arak
The Veiled Alliance's primary base of operations in the Tablelands. The city's Sorcerer-King Morthek is dying (or already dead — the city doesn't officially know yet). His Templars are running the city in his name.

Quests:
- **Find the Alliance Cell:** Not straightforward — three different "Alliance" groups exist; one is genuine, one is a Templar trap, one is a splinter faction with extremist methods
- **The Water Broker:** Desh-Arak controls the Deepwell of Karrath; someone is poisoning it slowly; finding who and why connects to the main ritual
- **Morthek's Secret:** If the party investigates the dying Sorcerer-King's private chambers, they find evidence that he knew about Kalak's ritual — and was trying to replicate it

#### Gulg (New Content — Restored)
In the original game, Gulg was referenced but minimally explored. The remake completes it:
- **The Oba's Forest:** Lalali-Puy has maintained her forest through genuine Preservation — and she knows things about the elemental balance that no one else does
- **The Audience:** Getting an audience with Lalali-Puy requires completing a trial; the trial tests whether the party is worthy of her attention, not their combat ability
- **The Bargain:** Lalali-Puy will help stop Kalak's ritual — but her price involves preserving her forest from a Defiler threat. Her motives are ultimately self-serving but her information is accurate.

#### Draj (New Content)
Tectuktitlay's Draj was barely present in the original. The remake adds:
- **The Moon Priest Circuit:** Draj's theocracy has noticed unusual behavior at one of the ritual anchor points; they think it's a religious event; the party can use this confusion
- **The Sacrifice Question:** Draj's sacrifice system is functional and ongoing. The party will witness it. They can intervene (costly; Tectuktitlay's wrath) or not (cheap; morally weighted).
- **The Underground:** A slave resistance network in Draj connects to the faction reputation system

### Act Two: Wilderness Content
Between city-states, the Tablelands offer:
- **The Sandrunner Accord** (Elf questline — restored/new)
- **The Bonefield** (Salt Wars ruins; historical lore; treasure and danger)
- **The Obsidian Oracle** (A psionic entity trapped in the Obsidian Shelf; optional; rewards deep psionic lore and a unique power)
- **The Last Preserver** (An ancient Preserver wizard, centuries old, hiding in the deep wastes; knows about Rajaat; extreme late-Act 2 content)

### Act Two Ending
The party locates all three anchor points and understands what the ritual will do if completed: drain the life from the entire Tyr Region — not just the land but every living thing in it, including the city-states. The Dragon (Borys) is also aware of the ritual — and he wants the power for himself. He is moving. Act Three begins.

## Act Three — The Ritual

### The Final Convergence
The party must reach the ritual's **Nexus Point** — deep beneath the ruins of a pre-Cataclysm city — and either:
1. **Destroy the Nexus:** Stops the ritual; the accumulated energy is released as a massive defilement event (devastating but localized)
2. **Redirect the Nexus:** Uses the Veiled Alliance's ritual knowledge to redirect the energy into restoring a section of Athas's ecology — extremely dangerous; requires a Preserver wizard in the party or a successful Arcane Lore check
3. **Complete the Ritual:** The party can choose to complete Kalak's metamorphosis — transforming one party member into a Dragon-equivalent. The most powerful ending. The most morally catastrophic.
4. **Give the Nexus to Borys:** The Dragon leaves the Tyr Region alone forever; the rest of Athas pays more

### The Dragon Encounter
Borys arrives at the Nexus regardless of the player's plan. This is not a fight — it is a negotiation, a series of choices under extreme duress, with the most powerful being on Athas demanding what he wants.

The party can:
- **Stand aside** — Borys takes the power; the Dragon problem gets worse globally
- **Negotiate** — requires extremely high faction relationships (Veiled Alliance, specific NPC connections); Borys agrees to a limited withdrawal
- **Distract/Delay** — buy time for another option; extremely costly in lives
- **The Psionic Gambit** — a Psionicist at high level can attempt to disrupt Borys's concentration; this does not defeat him, but it opens a window

### Act Three Key Locations
- **The Ruins of Ur-Draxa (analog):** The sunken pre-Cataclysm city; massive dungeon; multiple approaches
- **The Nexus Chamber:** Final location; visually the inverse of the opening arena — instead of sand and blood, it is stone and light
- **Kalak's Memory:** A psychic imprint of Kalak persists in the Nexus; this is the closest thing to a "final boss dialogue" — understanding why he did this, and what he was afraid of

## Unfinished Business — Restored Content

The following content was cut from the original game or left incomplete. The remake completes it:

| Original Gap | Remake Resolution |
|---|---|
| Elven tribe conflict hinted at but unresolved | **Sandrunner Accord** questline (full Act 2 quest) |
| Half-Elf companion Raaen mentioned in design docs, cut | Fully implemented companion with personal quest |
| Gulg almost entirely offscreen | Full city-state segment in Act 2 |
| Draj referenced but not visitable | Full city-state segment in Act 2 |
| The Dragon's motivation left vague | Borys's full backstory and motivation revealed in Act 3 |
| Kalak's death aftermath unexplored | Act 2 Tyr content focuses entirely on this |
| Water Cleric mechanics underpowered | Fully redesigned Water domain (see 06_magic.md) |
| Half-Elf racial identity had no mechanical or narrative support | Restored companion, racial dialogue, and unique trait |
| Nibenay's psionic research referenced in item text, never developed | Act 2 sidequest resolves this (implications for the main plot) |
| Wake of the Ravager's open ending | The remake's endings close the loop canonically |

## Factions

### 1. The Veiled Alliance
Underground network of Preserver wizards and sympathizers operating across all city-states. Not uniformly heroic — internal factions include pacifists, saboteurs, and extremists.

**Goal:** Overthrow the Sorcerer-Kings; restore Preservation as the dominant arcane practice.

**Reputation Rewards:**
- 20+: Access to safe houses in each city-state
- 40+: Alliance members will assist in combat (summoned ally once per city)
- 60+: Ritual spell training from master Preservers
- 80+: Full Alliance support in Act 3; unique ending available

**Key NPC:** Agis of Asticles (canonical DS character) — a Preserver noble who serves as the party's primary Alliance contact. His arc involves him confronting the extremist faction within the Alliance that he leads.

### 2. The Merchant Houses
Three major Houses (Wavir, Shom, Inika) control inter-city-state trade. They value profit, neutrality, and contracts above everything.

**Goal:** Maintain trade routes; maximize profit; survive regardless of political upheaval.

**Reputation Rewards:**
- 20+: 10% discount at House trading posts
- 40+: Access to restricted goods (rare materials, poisons, psionic items)
- 60+: House caravan escort (safe travel through one dangerous region per act)
- 80+: Intelligence network — the party receives advance warning of one major plot event

**Key NPC:** Merchant-Mistress Yal of House Wavir — pragmatic, honest about her self-interest, and more useful than she appears.

### 3. The Templars
The Sorcerer-Kings' administrative network. After Kalak's death, Tyr's Templars have lost their power source. The Templars of other city-states remain fully functional.

**Goal:** Maintain order; serve their king; survive the political chaos of Kalak's death.

**Reputation Rewards (positive):**
- High Templar reputation allows passage through checkpoints without stops
- Templar contacts provide information on city-state politics
- Defilers are given controlled defilement licenses (unusual but possible)

**Note:** High positive Templar reputation locks the player out of certain Veiled Alliance quests and vice versa. These factions are mutually incompatible at high relationship levels.

### 4. The Elemental Cults
Four sub-factions (Earth, Air, Fire, Water) united in opposition to defilement. Often in conflict with each other on doctrine. Elemental clerics draw from these networks.

**Goal:** Protect Athas's elemental fabric; restore elemental nodes; oppose defilement.

**Reputation Rewards:**
- 20+: Access to cult-maintained water sources (safe oases)
- 40+: Elemental node locations revealed on map
- 60+: Cult members join as temporary combat allies during elemental events
- 80+: The Four Winds ability — once per long rest, invoke all four elements for a massive area effect

### 5. The Slave Population
Not an organized faction — the enslaved people of the city-states. Represented as a diffuse reputation track.

**Goal:** Survival. Freedom, for those who dare dream of it.

**Reputation Rewards:**
- 20+: Slaves pass information freely to the party; reduced cost at black market traders
- 40+: Underground escape network assists party in cities (alternative routes, hiding spots)
- 60+: Slave uprisings can be triggered in cities as a distraction — but the party cannot control what happens to the people involved
- 80+: In Act 3, a network of freed slaves provides a supply chain to the Nexus

## Named Companions

### Rikus (Canonical)
**Race:** Mul | **Class:** Gladiator/Fighter | **Role:** Tank; frontline fighter
**Background:** The gladiator who organized the escape. Has been enslaved since birth. Anger is his primary operating mode; loyalty to people who respect him is his core value.
**Personal Quest:** Finding his original family, sold to a different city-state decades ago.
**Loyalty Triggers:** Values direct action over politics. Loses loyalty when the party negotiates with Templars or ignores an opportunity to free slaves.

### Neeva (Canonical)
**Race:** Human | **Class:** Fighter (Champion) | **Role:** Combat leader; team motivator
**Background:** Former arena champion; chose slavery over a complicated freedom. Haunted by that choice.
**Personal Quest:** Confronting the gladiatorial trainer who broke her will years ago.
**Loyalty Triggers:** Values courage and honesty. Loses loyalty when the party abandons NPCs in danger.

### Agis (Canonical)
**Race:** Human | **Class:** Psionicist | **Role:** Psionic support; political navigator
**Background:** A noble and Veiled Alliance member. The most politically sophisticated companion. His morality is more complicated than he presents.
**Personal Quest:** Discovering that his late wife was an informant for the Templars — and deciding what that means.
**Loyalty Triggers:** Values long-term strategy over short-term gains. Loses loyalty when the party takes destructive actions that harm innocent bystanders.

### Sadira (Canonical)
**Race:** Half-Elf | **Class:** Wizard (starts Preserver; can become Defiler through her quest arc)
**Role:** Arcane magic; the moral ambiguity character
**Background:** Veiled Alliance operative who has been questioning her own pacifist principles. Her personal arc is the game's central moral argument.
**Personal Quest:** Accessing an ancient cache of Defiler spells — and deciding whether to use them.
**Loyalty Triggers:** Values results. Can be pushed toward either Preservation or Defilement based on player choices; this is the most reactive companion arc.

### Raaen (Restored — was cut from original)
**Race:** Half-Elf | **Class:** Thief/Ranger | **Role:** Scout; infiltration; social skills
**Background:** A half-elf who has built an identity entirely around not needing anyone. The loneliest person in the party.
**Personal Quest:** Finding a tribe — elven or human — that will accept her. The outcome is deliberately not guaranteed.
**Loyalty Triggers:** Values honesty about her situation. Loses loyalty when the party pretends everything is fine or ignores social injustice.

### Korvix (New)
**Race:** Thri-Kreen | **Class:** Ranger/Psionicist | **Role:** Scout; psionic offense
**Background:** A Thri-Kreen who has spent 10 years in humanoid cities trying to understand them. He has not succeeded. He is trying anyway.
**Personal Quest:** Resolving a conflict between his former clutch (who want him to return to the wastes) and his chosen identity.
**Loyalty Triggers:** Values consistency. Loses loyalty when the party acts differently in private than in public.

## The Sorcerer-Kings as Characters

The Sorcerer-Kings are not boss fights. They are ancient intelligences operating on a timescale the player cannot fully grasp. Each one the player encounters should feel like interacting with something genuinely alien to normal human experience.

### Hamanu of Urik
The most militaristic. Genuinely believes that order, even brutal order, is preferable to the chaos of Athas without city-states. He may be right. The party will encounter him through politics, not combat.

### Lalali-Puy of Gulg
The most interesting moral case: she preserved a forest. She is also a sadistic tyrant. The coexistence of these facts is the point.

### Tectuktitlay of Draj
The most theatrical. His religious performance is sincere — he genuinely believes in his own mythology at this point. Three thousand years of being worshipped does something to a mind.

### Kalak of Tyr (Dead — but Present)
His absence is a character. Tyr's entire political situation, the main plot, and two companion arcs all orbit what he did and why. His psychic impression in the Nexus is the closest thing to meeting him.

## Endings

### Ending A: The Sacrifice (Destroy the Nexus)
The ritual is stopped by destroying the Nexus. The accumulated energy is released as a catastrophic defilement event that kills everything within 20 miles. The Tyr Region survives. The cost is enormous. Epilogue: the party scatters; Athas continues its slow death, slightly slower.

### Ending B: The Restoration (Redirect the Nexus)
Requires: Preserver wizard in party (Sadira or player Preserver character) + Veiled Alliance relationship 60+. The energy is redirected. A 20-mile radius around the Nexus becomes the first new forest on Athas in centuries. The Dragon notices. The epilogue is hopeful but not safe.

### Ending C: The Dragon's Bargain (Give the Nexus to Borys)
Borys takes the power. The Tyr Region is spared from his tribute for one generation. The rest of Athas gets worse faster. Epilogue: the party is alive; Athas is closer to death; the party knows exactly what they chose.

### Ending D: The Metamorphosis (Complete the Ritual)
One party member undergoes the transformation. They become something between human and Dragon — vast power, vast cost, losing pieces of themselves with every use of that power. The Tyr Region is protected. Athas has a new Dragon. Epilogue: the others leave. The transformed character remains, alone, at the Nexus. The most complete ending. The most terrible one.
